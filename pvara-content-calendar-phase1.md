# 3. GAPS, AMBIGUITIES AND CONFLICTS

## 3.1 The single most important issue: section numbering across documents does not match

Both subordinate instruments are drafted against the **Virtual Assets Ordinance, 2025**, not the Act as passed. The section numbers do not line up. This is not a small thing — it affects almost every cross-reference a reader will try to follow.

| Instrument says | Act 2026 equivalent | Note |
|---|---|---|
| NOC Regs 2.2, 15.3(b), 15.4 — "Section 15 of the Ordinance" (NOC / incorporation) | s.19 | Same substance, different number |
| NOC Regs 2.3, 15.3(c), 17.1(ii) — "Section 17 of the Ordinance" (licence) | s.21 | Same substance, different number |
| NOC Regs 6.1 — "section 16 of the Act" (fit and proper) | s.20 | Also inconsistently calls it "the Act" not "the Ordinance" |
| NOC Regs 2.3 — "Section 38(1) of the Ordinance" (DNFBP designation) | **No equivalent found.** Act s.38 is the Strategic Digital Wallet Company | See 3.2 below |
| Sandbox s.1, Introduction — "Sections 42 to 45 of the Ordinance" (sandbox, exit, no-action) | s.35 covers all of it in one section | The Act consolidates four sections into one |
| Sandbox s.1 — "Section 6, sub-section 1" (establishment of Authority) | s.6(1) | Matches |
| Sandbox s.1 — "section 8 the Ordinance" (the "Board") | s.7 (composition) / s.8 (procedures) | The Act never uses the word "Board" for PVARA's governing body; it says "the Authority" |

**How we handle it in the articles:** always cite the instrument's own numbering *as the instrument states it*, then give the Act 2026 equivalent in a parenthetical, and flag where no equivalent exists. Never silently renumber. I recommend one dedicated article on this (**198**) so we have a single canonical explainer to link to from every affected piece.

## 3.2 Substantive conflicts I could not resolve from the documents

**(a) The NOC Regulations permit pre-licence trading; the Act appears to prohibit it.**
NOC Regs reg 2.3 states that an applicant granted AML Registration "may provide AML-Registered Services prior to obtaining a license" — that is, exchange, broker-dealer, custody and derivatives services. Act s.50(1) states that no person shall provide any Virtual Asset Service unless that person is a Pakistani-incorporated company **and** holds a valid licence. Act s.54(1) makes unlicensed provision a five-year, Rs 50 million offence. Act s.70 provides a different transitional route (six months to apply, continue while the application is pending).

These are not obviously reconcilable on the face of the documents. Possible readings: the NOC pathway operates as an exercise of the s.70 transitional discretion; or the NOC Regulations were valid under the Ordinance and s.74 savings preserve them; or the position has changed and the NOC Regulations need updating. **The documents do not say.** I will not assert any of these readings as fact. Articles 062, 063 and 127 will state the tension plainly and say the position should be confirmed with PVARA.

**(b) The "Section 38(1)" DNFBP designation has no home in the Act.**
NOC Regs reg 2.3 designates the four AML-Registered Services as "non-financial businesses and professions in accordance with Section 38(1) of the Ordinance." Act s.46(1) does the opposite — it deems VASPs to be **financial institutions** for AMLA 2010 purposes. Designating a service as a DNFBP and deeming the provider a financial institution are different regulatory treatments under the AML framework. I cannot tell from these documents which governs. Flagged in 062 and 073.

**(c) Two different Key Individual lists.**
Act s.3(1)(xv) lists ten positions including Managing Director, COO and head of information-security. NOC Regs reg 5.1 lists eight, adding **Chief Executive Officer** (absent from the Act's list) and omitting Managing Director, COO and head of internal audit... actually it retains head of internal audit but omits Managing Director and COO. Act s.20(1) separately names "Chief Executive Officer" as subject to fit-and-proper assessment, so the CEO is clearly in scope somewhere. The lists simply do not match. Articles 033 and 066 will present both lists side by side rather than merging them.

**(d) The administrative penalty ceiling appears twice, differently.**
Act s.59(1)(c) allows a financial penalty "up to the maximum amount prescribed by the Rules." Act s.59(4) says the Authority "may impose a fine up to twenty-five million Rupees for any contravention." Whether s.59(4) is a hard cap on s.59(1)(c) or a separate freestanding power is not stated. Flagged in 137.

**(e) A drafting inconsistency in the closed-loop exclusion.**
Act s.2(2)(a) says a token must satisfy "**all** of the following conditions," then lists (i) through (vii) joined by "**or**" before the final limb. Conjunctive and disjunctive readings give very different scopes. Articles 023 will quote the provision exactly and state that the reading is unsettled.

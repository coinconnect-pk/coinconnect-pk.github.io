Part A of Form I lets [PVARA](https://www.coinconnect.site/pvara-guide) judge whether a [sandbox](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-sandbox-reduced-capital-pakistan-19) applicant's product is genuinely innovative. Part B asks a different question entirely: even if the idea is sound, is the applicant actually ready to run it live, today, under supervision? The Sandbox Guidelines 2026 treat readiness as a separate, evidence-based test, and Part B is where that evidence has to be shown.

This article works through the four areas of Part B, plus the standalone product-detail requirement that sits alongside them, and explains how each connects to the eligibility criteria an applicant already had to meet.

## What is Form I Part B, and how is it different from Part A?

Form I Part B, headed "Readiness for Testing", asks an applicant to demonstrate that its technology, partnerships, funding and consumer safeguards are actually built and working, whereas Part A asks only for a description of what the innovation is. The Guidelines set out four numbered areas plus one further product-specific requirement:

| Area | What to show | Format | Attachment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Technical readiness | Development status, internal testing/testnet results, capacity | Yes/No + short description | No |
| 2. Integration / partnerships | List of partners (banks, exchanges, VASPs), integration points | Table/bullets | No |
| 3. Operational & financial readiness | Budget, funding commitments, operational structures, safeguards, KPIs/KRIs | Bullets/table | No |
| 4. Consumer / user safety | Protection measures, risk disclosure, loss recovery, support, dispute resolution | Narrative + key points | No |
| Virtual asset product detail (where applicable) | Product details in terms of Virtual Asset Standards | Narrative + table + key points | Yes |

Notably, none of the four core Part B areas calls for a separate attachment — the Guidelines expect the evidence itself embedded in the form, in the Yes/No and short-description, table and narrative formats specified, rather than deferred to appendices. Only the standalone [virtual asset](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-license-pakistan-complete-guide-22) product-detail item requires an attachment.

## What counts as technical readiness under section 1?

Technical readiness means showing the actual development status of the product, evidence from internal testing or a testnet, and the system's capacity to handle the planned test — answered directly as Yes/No with a short supporting description, not as a narrative essay. The Guidelines specify: "Development status (smart contracts audited, infrastructure ready), internal testing/testnet results, capacity."

The parenthetical detail matters: "smart contracts audited" is named specifically, which means an applicant whose product relies on smart contracts should expect the absence of an audit to be read as a readiness gap, not a minor omission. This connects directly back to the [cybersecurity strategy](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-sandbox-form-i-complete-walkthrough-2026-8) claimed in Part A section 3 — a Part A submission that describes audit practices in the abstract needs Part B evidence that an audit actually happened before testing starts.

## What must the integration and partnerships section show?

Section 2 asks for a list of the applicant's actual partners — banks, exchanges, other virtual asset service providers — and the specific integration points connecting the applicant's system to each, such as APIs or oracles, set out as a table or bullet list. The Guidelines describe this as: "List of partners (banks, exchanges, VASPs), integration points (APIs, oracles)."

This section is where a sandbox application either proves or fails to prove that the product can function in Pakistan's actual financial infrastructure, not just in isolation. A [virtual asset service provider](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/vasp-license-pakistan-3) that lists prospective partners without a working integration in place is describing a plan, not readiness, and the eligibility criteria's requirement for "readiness for scalability (technical, financial, and human)" assumes the underlying integrations already exist rather than being negotiated during the test period itself.

## What operational and financial evidence does section 3 require?

Section 3 asks for the budget behind the test, evidence of funding actually committed, the operational structures and safeguards in place, and the key performance and risk indicators (KPIs/KRIs) the applicant will track, set out as bullets or a table. The Guidelines list: "Budget, funding commitments, operational structures, safeguards, KPIs/KRIs."

This section is the direct evidentiary counterpart to the "Financial Strength" limb of the key evaluation criteria, which requires "demonstration of financial capacity to undertake the proposed business model." KPIs and KRIs deserve particular care here, because section 1 of the eligibility criteria separately requires "a clearly defined testing plan (objectives, duration, KPIs, and target users)" at application stage — the figures given in Part B section 3 should be the same figures the testing plan commits to, not a second, inconsistent set.

## What consumer safety evidence does section 4 require?

Section 4 asks the applicant to show, in narrative form with key points highlighted, the actual measures in place to protect consumers during testing — risk disclosure, loss recovery arrangements, customer support, and how disputes are resolved. The Guidelines describe this as: "Protection measures (risk disclosure, loss recovery, customer support, dispute resolution)."

This is the readiness-stage evidence for a commitment the applicant already made under the eligibility criteria, which requires "risk management and consumer protection measures (including data security, dispute resolution, and safeguarding consumer assets)" before an application can even be considered. It is also the evidence base a reviewer will return to later if the Authority ever has to assess a suspension linked to consumer detriment during testing — what section 4 promises here becomes the benchmark for what actually has to run once the sandbox is live.

## Where does the virtual asset product detail requirement fit in?

Separate from the four numbered areas, Part B carries a further requirement — where the applicant is offering a virtual asset product specifically — to describe that product's details "in terms of Virtual Asset Standards", presented as narrative, table and key points together, and this is the only item in Part B that requires a supporting attachment. The Guidelines do not define "Virtual Asset Standards" further within the text of the Sandbox Guidelines themselves; an applicant should confirm with PVARA directly what standards document, if any, currently governs this description before drafting it, rather than assuming a specific format.

## How does Part B feed into PVARA's evaluation?

Part B's four areas map directly onto two of Form I's stated evaluation criteria — "Operational Capacity and Readiness for Testing" and "Safeguards for User Protection and Consumer Trust" — and the submission checklist repeats the same items individually: a technical readiness statement with testing history, partnerships and integrations detail, financial readiness with KPIs and KRIs, and consumer protection and user safety measures. Because assessment proceeds on "submission at the time of the application or any other information sought subsequently", gaps identified in Part B are the kind of thing PVARA can and does come back to ask about before the 60-working-day evaluation clock runs out.

Applicants preparing a submission through a [regulatory and licensing](https://www.coinconnect.site/regulatory-licensing) adviser typically build Part B alongside Part A rather than after it, precisely because the two are meant to be read together — Part A's technology and cybersecurity claims only carry weight once Part B shows the audit, the testnet result, or the partnership integration that proves them.

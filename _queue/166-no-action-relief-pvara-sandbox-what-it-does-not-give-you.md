Founders preparing a [sandbox](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-sandbox-reduced-capital-pakistan-19) application sometimes treat a no-action letter as a shield — a piece of paper that puts enforcement risk to one side for the life of the test. The Sandbox Guidelines 2026 describe something narrower and more conditional than that. It is worth reading the actual wording closely before relying on it in a business plan.

This article sets out what a no-action letter covers, what it explicitly does not give a participant, [how long](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/3/how-long-pvara-license-takes-39) it runs, and where [PVARA](https://www.coinconnect.site/pvara-guide)'s power to issue one comes from.

## What is no-action relief under the PVARA sandbox?

No-action relief is a letter from PVARA stating that it does not intend to take enforcement action against specified conduct, for a defined period, while a participant is testing in the sandbox. The Guidelines define it as:

> "'No-Action Relief' means a no-action letter issued by the Authority under Section 45 of the Ordinance, indicating that the Authority does not intend to take enforcement action in respect of specified conduct for a defined period."

Section 14 of the Guidelines, headed "No-Action Relief", restates this operationally: "The Authority may issue a no-action letter to the participants, stating that it does not intend to take enforcement action in respect of specified conduct for the duration of the test period." Two words carry the weight here — **specified** and **duration**. Relief attaches to conduct PVARA has identified in the letter, not to the participant's business generally, and it runs for the test period, not indefinitely.

## What does a no-action letter actually cover?

A no-action letter tells a participant that PVARA will not use its own enforcement powers against the exact conduct named in the letter, for as long as testing continues. It is a statement of PVARA's own enforcement intent, addressed to the specific activity the participant disclosed when applying — for example, running a novel token structure or a [custody](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-custody-license-safeguarding-customer-assets-25) model that would otherwise sit outside the participant's existing authorisation.

Because it is scoped to "specified conduct", anything not named in the letter falls outside it. A participant that expands the tested product, changes its structure, or adds features beyond what was described in its [Form I](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-sandbox-form-i-complete-walkthrough-2026-8) application should not assume the same letter still applies — the Guidelines give no basis for reading it that broadly.

## What does a no-action letter not give a sandbox participant?

A no-action letter is not legal immunity, and PVARA can withdraw it at any time on written notice — it is a revocable statement of enforcement intent, not a legal shield. The Guidelines are explicit:

> "the issuance of a no-action letter shall not constitute a legal immunity, and the Authority reserves the right to withdraw such a letter at any time by providing written notice."

Reading this alongside the rest of the framework, our reading is that a no-action letter does not:

- Prevent enforcement by any regulator other than PVARA — it says nothing about the State Bank of Pakistan, the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan, the Federal Board of Revenue, or the Financial Monitoring Unit, each of which retains its own separate powers.
- Displace obligations under Pakistan's [anti-money laundering](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/3/fit-and-proper-aml-pvara-mistakes-54) framework or the ML/TF/PF risk controls the participant committed to at application stage.
- Protect against a private civil claim brought by a customer or counterparty — the letter speaks to the Authority's own enforcement intent, not to third-party rights.
- Survive a change in the underlying conduct — relief is tied to what was "specified", and PVARA can withdraw even that on notice.
- Guarantee that the tested product will later be licensed — the Guidelines treat no-action relief and eventual licensing as separate questions entirely.

## How long does no-action relief last, and can PVARA end it early?

No-action relief runs for the duration of the approved test period and ends when testing ends, but PVARA can also withdraw it earlier than that, at any time, simply by giving written notice. There is no cure period, appeal mechanism or minimum notice length specified in the published Guidelines for that withdrawal — a gap worth flagging rather than filling with an assumption.

This puts no-action relief in the same category as the sandbox approval itself: a benefit conditional on continued good standing, not a fixed-term contractual right. A participant that faces suspension or withdrawal of its own sandbox approval should assume any accompanying no-action letter is equally exposed, since both sit on the same supervisory relationship.

## Where does PVARA's power to issue no-action relief come from?

The Guidelines define no-action relief by reference to "Section 45 of the Ordinance", but the [Virtual Assets Act](https://blog.coinconnect.site/pakistans-virtual-assets-act-2026-the-complete-guide/), 2026 — the instrument actually passed by the National Assembly — carries the power to issue no-action communications at a different location. Section 35(3) of the Act provides that "the Authority may issue guidance, no-objection statements or no-action communications in accordance with Regulations", within the same section that establishes the sandbox itself. The Act's own section 45 deals with a separate subject, complaint-handling and dispute resolution, and has nothing to do with no-action relief.

This is not a small drafting slip to skim past. Our reading is that the Guidelines were drafted against an earlier numbering of the underlying law and have not yet been updated to match the Act as passed, and that section 74 of the Act — a savings clause preserving anything done under the lapsed Ordinance as if done under the Act's "corresponding provisions" — carries the Guidelines' cross-references across to section 35(3). Confirm the current section reference with PVARA directly before citing it in a formal submission; do not rely on either number in isolation.

## How does no-action relief sit alongside the rest of the sandbox toolkit?

No-action relief is one of three distinct protections PVARA can extend to a sandbox participant, and it is the narrowest of the three: the Letter of Approval authorises the test itself, the sandbox undertaking sets the participant's obligations while testing runs, and the no-action letter separately limits PVARA's own enforcement response to specified conduct within that test. None of the three substitutes for another.

A participant weighing whether to apply for the [sandbox](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-sandbox-reduced-capital-pakistan-19) rather than pursuing a [no objection certificate](https://www.coinconnect.site/regulatory-licensing) or full licence directly should treat no-action relief as a narrow, revocable comfort attached to a specific test — useful for de-risking one identified regulatory question, not a substitute for the [licensing framework](https://www.coinconnect.site/pvara-guide) the product will eventually need. Our comparison of [sandbox, NOC, no-action and full licence](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-routes-compared-sandbox-noc-license-20) routes sets out how the four sit against each other in more detail.

## What should a sandbox applicant actually ask PVARA for in a no-action request?

An applicant should ask PVARA to name the specific conduct it wants covered as precisely as the product description in its Form I application, rather than requesting a general comfort letter, because relief only ever attaches to what the letter itself specifies. A request framed around a single, clearly defined activity — a particular custody arrangement, a particular token structure, a particular transaction type — gives PVARA something concrete to grant, and gives the participant a clear boundary to test against once relief is issued. A vague request invites either a vague letter, which protects little, or a refusal, since the Authority has nothing specific to evaluate.

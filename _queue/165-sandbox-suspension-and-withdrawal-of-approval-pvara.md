[Sandbox](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-sandbox-reduced-capital-pakistan-19) approval is not a fixed grant. Once the Pakistan [Virtual Asset](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-license-pakistan-complete-guide-22) Regulatory Authority ([PVARA](https://www.coinconnect.site/pvara-guide)) has issued a Letter of Approval and a participant is testing live, the Authority keeps a standing power to pull that testing back — either for a period, or permanently. The Sandbox Guidelines 2026 set this out in a single short section, but the consequences for a participant reach into every part of how testing is run.

This article looks at what triggers suspension, what triggers withdrawal, how the two differ from the separate termination clause in the sandbox undertaking, and what a participant should do to keep the gap between them wide.

## What power does PVARA have to suspend or withdraw sandbox approval?

PVARA can pause a participant's testing at any stage, or end it permanently, if it has reason to believe the participant has broken the terms it agreed to. The Sandbox Guidelines state:

> "At any stage, where Authority has possible reasons to believe that the participant has failed to adhere to the details agreed or the conditions imposed during the review and selection stage: the Authority can temporarily suspend the testing and approval until the matter in question has been fully clarified; the Authority can completely withdraw the approval with a public notice in case a serious discrepancy has been observed related to consumer detriment or any other serious matter."

Two things stand out in that wording. First, the trigger is a belief that "possible reasons" exist — not a finding of fact. The Authority does not need to have concluded wrongdoing before it acts; it needs only a basis to suspect a departure from the agreed terms. Second, "at any stage" means the power runs for the whole life of the sandbox period, from the day the Letter of Approval issues to the day testing is due to close.

## How is suspension different from withdrawal?

Suspension is a temporary pause pending clarification; withdrawal is a permanent end to approval, made public, and reserved for serious consumer-related discrepancies. The Guidelines treat them as two distinct remedies of different severity, not two names for the same event.

| | Suspension | Withdrawal |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | Temporary, "until the matter in question has been fully clarified" | Permanent |
| Threshold | Possible reasons to believe a term or condition was breached | "Serious discrepancy" tied to consumer detriment or another serious matter |
| Publicity | Not stated as public | Made with a **public notice** |
| Effect | Testing and approval paused | Approval ends |

Our reading is that suspension functions as an investigative hold: PVARA freezes testing while it establishes the facts, and the participant's status is unresolved until it does. Withdrawal is the outcome once PVARA has concluded the discrepancy is serious enough — the Guidelines name consumer detriment specifically, then add "any other serious matter" as a catch-all, without defining what else qualifies. That catch-all is broad and undefined in the published text; treat any communication from the Authority that invokes it as a matter for immediate legal advice, not routine correspondence.

## How does withdrawal differ from termination under the sandbox undertaking?

Withdrawal under the Guidelines is a regulatory finding of serious discrepancy; termination under the Annexure-B undertaking is a contractual exit right PVARA holds regardless of fault. They can produce the same practical result — the participant stops testing — but they are legally distinct routes to get there, and a participant should not assume the safeguards of one apply to the other.

The undertaking a participant signs on approval gives PVARA a separate, broader termination right:

> "It agrees and acknowledges that the Authority may terminate its participation in the Regulatory Sandbox by giving written notice at least 15 days prior to the termination date, or immediately in the following circumstances: Breach of the testing plan; Deployment of the proposition is expected to have negative consequences for the consumers and/or overall financial stability or failure to provide requested information; Public interest."

Notice the differences from section 13's suspension and withdrawal power:

- Termination carries a **15-day notice period** as the default, falling away only for the three listed immediate triggers.
- The immediate triggers are wider than "serious discrepancy" — they include a plain breach of the testing plan, an expected (not yet realised) negative consequence, or simply "public interest", none of which require the consumer-detriment threshold that withdrawal under section 13 sets.
- Termination sits in the contract the participant signs, alongside its own indemnity and insurance obligations, whereas suspension and withdrawal are supervisory powers stated in the Guidelines themselves.

A [regulatory and licensing](https://www.coinconnect.site/regulatory-licensing) team should map both routes before a participant signs the undertaking, because the practical exposure — an abrupt stop to a live product with customers on it — is the same whichever route PVARA uses.

## What happens to a participant during a suspension?

The published Guidelines do not describe the mechanics of a suspension in any further detail — no minimum or maximum duration, no defined procedure for the participant to respond, and no stated right to be heard before the pause takes effect. This is a genuine gap in the published text rather than an inference on our part, and it is the single most important thing a participant preparing for the sandbox should note.

In practice, a suspended participant should expect at minimum to:

1. Stop onboarding new test users while the matter is clarified.
2. Preserve all records relevant to the "details agreed or conditions imposed" — the same records the undertaking signed at [Form I](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-sandbox-form-i-complete-walkthrough-2026-8) approval stage already commits the participant to retaining for seven years and making available to the Authority on demand.
3. Respond to the Authority's request for clarification promptly, since the undertaking separately requires participants to notify PVARA within one hour of a material incident and to follow with a full report within 48 hours — a discipline worth applying to any suspension-related request even where the Guidelines do not name a specific deadline for that response.

## What should a sandbox applicant do to reduce this risk before it applies?

The clearest way to avoid suspension or withdrawal is to keep the testing plan, risk controls and reporting commitments made at application stage exactly aligned with what actually runs during testing, because both remedies are triggered by a departure from what was agreed, not by a new rule being broken. Every condition a participant accepts in its [Form I](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-sandbox-form-i-complete-walkthrough-2026-8) application and Letter of Approval becomes, in effect, the yardstick the Authority measures it against for the life of the sandbox.

Practical steps that follow from the text:

- Treat any change to the tested product, target users, transaction limits or partners as a change to the "details agreed", and raise it with PVARA before implementing it rather than after.
- Keep the consumer protection and complaint-handling arrangements described in the application actually running, not just documented — a "serious discrepancy" related to consumer detriment is the one route to withdrawal the Guidelines name explicitly, a theme our comparison of [sandbox, NOC, no-action and full licence](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-routes-compared-sandbox-noc-license-20) routes returns to.
- Keep the one-hour and 48-hour incident timelines live in the compliance calendar from day one of testing, not from the day an incident occurs.
- Take specialist advice at the first sign of a suspension notice; because the Guidelines set no defined process or minimum duration, how a participant responds is likely to shape the outcome more than the underlying facts alone.

## Flags for Malik

- The Guidelines give no minimum/maximum suspension duration, no defined response procedure and no stated right to be heard before suspension. I have flagged this as a gap rather than guessing at a process — worth confirming directly with PVARA if a client ever faces this in practice.
- "Any other serious matter" in the withdrawal trigger is undefined. I deliberately did not speculate on examples beyond consumer detriment, to avoid inventing categories PVARA has not published.
- I distinguished section 13 (suspension/withdrawal) from the Annexure-B termination clause because they are genuinely different powers with different notice periods and thresholds — worth checking this reads clearly to someone who has not seen article 017 (the general sandbox walkthrough) first, since this piece assumes some of that context.

Innovation is the first of the Key Evaluation Criteria the [PVARA](https://www.coinconnect.site/pvara-guide) [Sandbox](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-sandbox-reduced-capital-pakistan-19) Guidelines 2026 apply to a sandbox application, and it is not a single yes-or-no judgement. The Guidelines break it into four named factors, each testing a different aspect of what makes a proposition genuinely new rather than merely different in name.

This piece works through all four factors in the order the Guidelines list them, and separates this formal evaluation stage from the self-assessment an applicant completes before ever filing.

## What does PVARA mean by "Innovation and Market Impact"?

It is the first heading under the Guidelines' Key Evaluation Criteria section, applied during the Assessment Phase once an application has cleared initial screening. The Guidelines list four sub-factors under it: novelty of the model, harnessing technology, differentiation, and inclusion. Each is stated as a short definition in the source text, without a scoring formula or weighting attached — the Guidelines describe what each factor looks for, not how many points it is worth relative to the others.

## What is the "novelty of the model" factor?

A test of whether the market has seen this before. The Guidelines define it as "a product, service, or business model that is not currently offered in the market." Read narrowly, this is a factual question rather than a qualitative one — it asks whether an equivalent already exists in the Pakistani market, not whether the applicant's version is better than what exists. An applicant proposing a product with no direct equivalent currently offered meets this factor on its own terms; an applicant proposing a variant of something already available needs to look instead at the differentiation factor below, since novelty of the model is not where that case gets made.

## What does "harnessing technology" test for?

Two distinct routes to qualifying, not one. The Guidelines state this factor as "a new application of an existing technology or a completely new, ground breaking technology." The first route does not require inventing new technology at all — applying an established technology (a particular blockchain architecture, a particular smart contract pattern) to a use case it has not previously served in the Pakistani market satisfies this route. The second route is the higher bar: technology that is itself new and ground-breaking, independent of the use case it serves. An applicant only needs to satisfy one of the two routes, not both.

## What counts as sufficient "differentiation"?

More than a change of branding. The Guidelines define differentiation as requiring that "the innovation represents a significant departure from or improvement on existing offerings addressing market inefficiencies." Three elements sit inside that single sentence: the departure or improvement must be significant, not incremental; it must be measured against existing offerings, meaning the applicant needs to be able to name what it is departing from or improving on; and it must address a market inefficiency, meaning the differentiation has to solve a real problem rather than simply look different for its own sake. An applicant that cannot point to a specific market inefficiency its proposition addresses has not made the case this factor asks for.

## What is the "inclusion" factor, and why does it single out informal markets?

It rewards formalising activity that already happens outside regulation. The Guidelines' own wording — reproduced here exactly as published, including its wording — defines this factor as: "A novation that helps transitions a largely informal and high-risk market into a formalized, regulated safe ecosystem and important pillar of the economy." Read past the source text's own phrasing, the substance of this factor is distinct from the other three: novelty, technology and differentiation all test the proposition against other formal market offerings, while inclusion instead tests it against informal activity that currently sits outside any regulatory perimeter — activity PVARA has an interest in bringing inside one. A proposition aimed squarely at formalising a segment of Pakistan's [virtual asset](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-license-pakistan-complete-guide-22) activity that currently operates informally has a distinct route to scoring well on this factor, separate from how novel or technologically advanced it is.

## How does the Innovation and Market Impact test compare with the Annexure-A self-assessment?

They overlap in subject but sit at different stages. Annexure-A's Genuine Innovation question, covered in a companion piece on this site, is a self-assessment an applicant completes before filing, with its own positive and negative indicators — a new use case not yet tested locally, or numerous similar models already existing in Pakistan, for instance. The Innovation and Market Impact factor covered here is the Guidelines' formal Key Evaluation Criteria heading, applied by PVARA during the Assessment Phase itself. The two are closely related in substance — an applicant who cannot honestly tick Annexure-A's positive indicator is very likely to struggle against the same four factors during formal assessment — but they are not the same document, and completing Annexure-A well is preparation for this stage, not a substitute for it.

## How does this evaluation factor relate to the other Key Evaluation Criteria?

Innovation and Market Impact is listed first among several headings the Guidelines group under Key Evaluation Criteria, alongside Risk Management and Compliance, Feasibility and Exit Strategy, Financial Strength, and [Tax](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/crypto-tax-pakistan-7) Law Compliance. The Guidelines do not state that Innovation and Market Impact is weighted more heavily than the others simply because it appears first, and this analysis does not assume an ordering of importance the text does not state. A companion piece on this site covers the risk, compliance and financial strength factors that sit alongside this one in the same evaluation stage.

## What should an applicant take from the four factors?

Address each of the four separately rather than treating "innovation" as one broad claim. A strong answer on novelty of the model does not carry over automatically to differentiation, since the two ask different questions — one about whether an equivalent exists, the other about whether the gap from existing offerings is significant and tied to a named market inefficiency. An applicant preparing the Innovation / [Virtual Asset Service Provider](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/vasp-license-pakistan-3) Proposition section of Form I should map its content against all four factors individually, including inclusion, which is easy to overlook if a proposition is being pitched primarily on its technology rather than on the informal market it stands to formalise.

A final observation. These four factors reward applicants who can describe what is genuinely new about their model in plain terms, and they punish those who cannot.

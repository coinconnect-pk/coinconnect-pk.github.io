Every participant in the [PVARA](https://www.coinconnect.site/pvara-guide) [sandbox](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-sandbox-reduced-capital-pakistan-19) eventually reaches the same moment: the test ends, and the Authority decides what comes next. The PVARA Sandbox Guidelines 2026 give that moment a defined term — Exit — and describe it as branching into one of three outcomes, not a single expected path from testing straight into a full licence.

This piece works through what Exit means as defined in the Guidelines, what determines which of the three outcomes a participant ends up on, and a genuine ambiguity in how the Guidelines cite the underlying statute that this analysis flags rather than resolves.

## How do the PVARA Sandbox Guidelines define "Exit"?

As a process with three possible destinations, not a single event. The Guidelines' Definitions section states that "'Exit' means the process, at the conclusion of Sandbox testing, through which a participant either transitions to full licensing, discontinues the [Virtual Asset](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-license-pakistan-complete-guide-22) service, or takes other steps as directed by the Authority." Read literally, the definition sets up three branches: full licensing, discontinuation, or a third, open-ended category of directed steps that is neither of the first two.

## What is the first outcome — transition to full licensing?

Moving from sandbox testing into a fully licensed [Virtual Asset Service Provider](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/vasp-license-pakistan-3), following a successful test. Annexure-B, the undertaking every approved participant signs, commits the participant to this specific condition: "It agrees that if the product or service is deemed successful, its rollout shall be subject to license/approval by the Authority and compliance with regulatory requirement, as issued from time to time." A successful sandbox test does not itself grant a licence — it is a gateway to a separate licensing process, subject to whatever regulatory requirements apply at that later point, which the Guidelines do not fix in advance.

## What is the second outcome — discontinuation?

Ceasing the Virtual Asset service that was being tested, where the test does not lead to a licence. The same undertaking requires the participant to agree that "it shall cease all sandbox activities upon expiration (if the testing is unsuccessful) or as required by the Authority." Discontinuation under the Exit definition and this cessation clause in Annexure-B describe the same practical outcome: the tested service stops, rather than continuing informally outside the sandbox structure that authorised it.

## What is the third outcome — "other steps as directed by the Authority"?

An open category the Guidelines do not narrow further in the text available. Beyond transition to full licensing and discontinuation, the Exit definition allows for the Authority to direct other steps entirely, without listing what those might be. Read against the Authority's general powers elsewhere in the Guidelines — including the power to impose case-by-case limits during testing, and to suspend or withdraw approval where a participant has failed to adhere to agreed terms — our reading is that this third branch gives the Authority room to respond to circumstances a simple binary of "pass" or "fail" would not capture, such as a test that succeeded technically but needs further conditions before scaling. The Guidelines do not confirm this reading; they simply leave the category open.

## Who decides which of the three outcomes applies?

The Authority, weighing the completion report against the actual testing results. The Guidelines' Exit Stage section states that "the Authority will analyse the testing phase results along with the completion report submitted by the participant at the exit stage and will determine the future course of action." Annexure-B reinforces that the participant does not control this outcome unilaterally: the participant agrees "to abide by and execute the exit strategy in accordance with the decision of the Authority and/or its own assessment of the test results at Exit Stage," and separately agrees that "the decision of Authority is final."

## Does a successful test guarantee a licence?

No. Annexure-B is explicit that the Authority is under no obligation to change the regulatory framework to accommodate a participant's product, and that any determination on regulatory changes is at the Authority's sole discretion. Even where a test is judged successful and the participant is directed toward full licensing, the undertaking separately requires the participant to "seek license or authorization from the Authority to operate commercially after successful conclusion of its participation in the sandbox, and comply with necessary regulatory requirements" — a further application and a further compliance standard, not an automatic conversion of sandbox approval into a licence.

## Where does the No-Action Relief provision fit alongside Exit?

As a separate, time-limited protection during testing, not part of the Exit decision itself. The Guidelines describe a no-action letter as one the Authority "may issue... to the participants, stating that it does not intend to take enforcement action in respect of specified conduct for the duration of the test period." The Guidelines are explicit that this "shall not constitute a legal immunity," and that the Authority "reserves the right to withdraw such a letter at any time by providing written notice." A no-action letter, where issued, protects specified conduct during the test itself; it is a distinct mechanism from the Exit determination that follows once testing has concluded.

## What genuine ambiguity does this raise, and why flag it rather than resolve it?

The Guidelines' Definitions section attributes the "other steps" branch of Exit to authority "under Section 44(3) of the Ordinance" — meaning the Virtual Assets Ordinance, 2025, which the Guidelines define as the underlying statute throughout. Pakistan's [Virtual Assets Act](https://blog.coinconnect.site/pakistans-virtual-assets-act-2026-the-complete-guide/), 2026 has since been passed, and its own savings provision states that action taken under the lapsed Ordinance "shall be deemed to have been validly done... under the corresponding provisions of this Act." The Act's own sandbox provision sits at a different section number than the 42–45 range the Guidelines cite for the Ordinance, and the source documents available for this analysis do not include a published correspondence table mapping old Ordinance sections to new Act sections. We have not guessed at what the "corresponding provision" is. Readers relying on the specific statutory citation behind the Exit definition should confirm current section numbering directly with PVARA rather than relying on the Ordinance-era citation reproduced in the Guidelines as published.

## What should a sandbox participant take from the three-outcome structure?

- Prepare for all three outcomes in the exit and communication planning required at application stage, not only for a successful transition to licensing.
- Treat "successful" testing as the start of a separate licensing application, not an automatic result — the undertaking requires a fresh application and continued compliance.
- Do not rely on a no-action letter as protection beyond the test period or beyond the specific conduct it names — it is expressly not immunity and can be withdrawn on notice.
- Build the completion report to support whichever outcome the actual results point toward, since the Authority weighs that report directly in reaching its determination.
- Confirm current statutory section references with PVARA directly where a document cites the lapsed Ordinance rather than the Virtual Assets Act, 2026.

## Flags for Malik

- This is the clearest Ordinance/Act numbering mismatch I have found in this batch: the Guidelines cite "Section 44(3) of the Ordinance" for the "other steps" branch of Exit, but the Virtual Assets Act, 2026 text I have access to does not carry a matching section 44, and its sandbox provision is a single section (35) rather than the 42–45 range the Guidelines cite. I have flagged this rather than guessed at a mapping — worth checking whether PVARA has issued updated Sandbox Guidelines referencing the Act directly.
- I have not speculated on what "other steps as directed by the Authority" might concretely mean beyond what the Guidelines' own powers elsewhere suggest, and marked that connection explicitly as a reading, not a stated rule.
- Sections referred to by heading name, not number, consistent with the rest of this batch, except where quoting the Guidelines' own numbered statutory citation directly.

A [sandbox](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/pvara-sandbox-reduced-capital-pakistan-19) application does not go from filed to decided in one step. The [PVARA](https://www.coinconnect.site/pvara-guide) Sandbox Guidelines 2026 split what happens after filing into two distinct stages, with a separate consultation step available for applicants that are already regulated elsewhere. Understanding where one stage ends and the other begins matters, because the sixty-working-day evaluation clock described elsewhere in the Guidelines only starts once the first stage is complete.

This piece works through both stages, the consultation power, and what information the Authority can draw on once assessment begins.

## What is the difference between initial screening and the Assessment Phase?

Initial screening checks that the application is complete; the Assessment Phase judges it on the merits. The Guidelines describe the gate between the two directly: "Applications passing the initial screening shall proceed to the Evaluation Phase." Screening is the documentary check covered in a companion piece on this site — confirming all required documents and information specified in the Guidelines are present — and it is a pass or fail on paperwork, not a judgement on the proposition itself. Only an application that clears screening moves forward to be judged against the six evaluation factors and the Key Evaluation Criteria.

## Which applications proceed to the Assessment Phase?

Any application that has passed initial screening, and only those. The Guidelines state this as a one-way gate — screening is the condition for entry into the Assessment Phase, not a formality that happens in parallel with it. An application still working through the two permitted resubmissions described in the Application submission process section has not yet cleared screening and has not yet reached this stage, whatever the underlying strength of the proposition.

## When does PVARA consult another regulator during assessment?

Only where the applicant is already regulated. The Assessment Phase section states: "If entities are regulated, the input from relevant regulator may be sought." Two things are worth reading carefully in that sentence. First, the trigger is the applicant's existing regulatory status, not the nature of the product being tested — an unregulated fintech proposing a genuinely novel product does not trigger this step, however innovative the proposition. Second, the word "may" makes this discretionary rather than mandatory: PVARA is not required to consult another regulator every time an applicant happens to be regulated, only empowered to do so.

The Guidelines do not name which regulators this covers, nor describe how the consultation is conducted, what it covers, or [how long](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/3/how-long-pvara-license-takes-39) it might add to the process. This analysis does not fill that gap with an assumption — an applicant that already holds a licence, registration or authorisation from another Pakistani regulator should treat the possibility of that regulator being consulted as a live one, without being able to predict its scope from the Guidelines alone.

## What information can PVARA draw on during assessment?

More than what was originally filed. The Guidelines' Assessment will cover section states: "Assessment will be conducted, based on submission at the time of the application or any other information sought subsequently in addition to already specified document/information, in order to assess the suitability of the sandbox applicant and viability of the products/services." This means the Assessment Phase is not a closed review of the original Form I package — the Authority can go back to the applicant for further information beyond what the Guidelines already specify as mandatory, and factor that into the same assessment.

## What is the assessment actually testing for?

The Guidelines frame the purpose in two parts, in the same sentence: "the suitability of the sandbox applicant and viability of the products/services." These are distinct tests. Suitability of the applicant covers matters such as the [fit and proper](https://www.coinconnect.site/blog/coinconnect-insights-1/url-slug-fit-and-proper-test-pvara-form-a3-14) standing, governance structure and operational readiness described in the eligibility criteria. Viability of the product or service is a separate question about whether the proposition itself — the innovation, the technology, the risk profile — stands up, independent of who is proposing it. An applicant can, in principle, be a suitable operator proposing a proposition that does not clear the viability bar, or vice versa; the Guidelines test both.

## Does "Assessment Phase" mean the same thing as "Evaluation Phase" in the Guidelines?

The published text uses both terms in close proximity without formally distinguishing them. The section heading reads "Assessment Phase," but the operative sentence beneath it refers to applications proceeding to the "Evaluation Phase," and a later section is headed "Timeline" and refers to "the comprehensive evaluation." Our reading is that these are the same stage described with two labels rather than two separate stages, since no second gate or handover point is described anywhere between them. An applicant should not read "Assessment Phase" and "Evaluation Phase" as sequential steps requiring separate clearance — nothing in the Guidelines describes them that way.

## What should an applicant do differently if it is already regulated elsewhere?

Disclose that status clearly and early in Form I's Applicant Particulars & Technical Details section, since it is the fact that can trigger the discretionary consultation power described above. Beyond that, the Guidelines do not describe a different process, a different set of documents, or a different evaluation standard for a regulated applicant compared with an unregulated one — the consultation power is additive to the standard Assessment Phase, not a substitute track. An applicant already holding standing with, for example, the State Bank of Pakistan or the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan should expect the same Form I, the same Annexure-A self-assessment, and the same Key Evaluation Criteria to apply, with the possibility of that other regulator's input sitting alongside PVARA's own review rather than replacing any part of it.

### A practical note on how to read this stage

The two phases reward different things, and applicants often prepare for only one of them.

Screening is a completeness exercise: it asks whether the application is capable of being assessed at all. Nothing you write about the merits of your product helps if a required element is missing.

Assessment is a merits exercise: it asks whether the proposition is one the Authority should supervise in a live environment. Completeness is assumed by that point and earns you nothing.

The practical consequence is that a strong application has to be built twice — once for the checker and once for the assessor. Applicants who write only for the assessor stall at the first gate; applicants who write only for the checker clear it and then have nothing to say.

## Flags for Malik

- The Guidelines switch between "Assessment Phase" and "Evaluation Phase" without formally reconciling the two terms. I read them as the same stage under two labels, marked clearly as "our reading" rather than as a stated fact — worth confirming with PVARA directly if the site needs a firmer answer.
- The Guidelines do not name which other regulators might be consulted under the "input from relevant regulator" power, or describe the consultation mechanics. I deliberately did not guess at names (SBP, SECP, etc.) as anything other than illustrative examples in the final section.
